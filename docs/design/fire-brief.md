# Fire: design brief (internal)

Reference research for the next Fire studies. Principles only: no imagery, characters, poses,
symbols, named techniques or scenes are reproduced anywhere on the site, and no franchise
terminology appears in the UI.

## 1. Directed-fire motion (animated martial-arts fire as a motion reference)

- **Force comes from outside the fire.** The fire extends an invisible force: it is launched,
  steered and released. It never just burns in place.
- **Short explosive impulses, not a steady stream.** Emission is a burst with a sharp onset.
- **Asymmetric timing.** Strikes are fast and hard; returns and drifts are soft and slow.
- **Breathe in, then out.** Gather (dim, contract, draw in) before a release.
- **Redirect in circles.** Direction changes by coiling, not by sharp angles.
- **Coherence reads as control.** Fire in motion should be tidy and laminar; turbulence grows only
  once control ends and the fire is left to dissipate.

## 2. Concentrated heat (restrained extreme heat as a visual concept)

- **Concentrate, don't spread.** Maximum power can be the smallest visible fire: a thin, intensely
  hot seam rather than a big flame.
- **Show heat by what it does to its surroundings.** Shimmer, parched haze, motes that lift and
  ignite near it.
- **Stillness against power.** Calm framing and slow change make the implied energy larger.
- **Too hot to look like flame.** The core goes to pale yellow-white, with almost no flame shape.

## 3. Natural and cinematic fire

- **Buoyancy drives motion** (roughly in proportion to temperature); fire cools quickly at first,
  then lingers (roughly T⁴ radiative loss).
- **Flicker from shed vortices:** a slow 1–3 Hz swell for large masses; faster flicker only on small
  tongues at the edges (pool fires puff at about 1.5/√D Hz).
- **Colour follows temperature:** deep red at the edges, then orange, amber, yellow, pale
  yellow-white at the hottest core. Cooling follows the same ramp back down to embers and smoke.
- **Blue is chemical:** only at a flame's base, in a clean gas jet or in a calm, fully burning core.
  Never at sooty plume tips.
- **Heat shimmer:** refractive-index gradients in rising hot air shift what is behind them many times
  a second.
- **What makes simulated fire read as fire:** fine detail at the flame edge, colour tied to
  temperature, and dissipation into embers and smoke.

## Combined principles for the new studies

1. **Anticipation:** a gather (embers drawn in, a glow contracting), 0.5–1 s.
2. **Ignition:** a sharp impulse that briefly overshoots to near-white, then settles to yellow-orange.
3. **Acceleration:** a fast start easing into travel (the strike).
4. **Directional travel:** curved arcs; change direction by coiling; low turbulence while
   controlled.
5. **Interaction / release:** a flare where the energy is let go; tongues shed and turbulence rises.
6. **Settling:** cooling along the temperature ramp, embers drifting, heat haze fading.
7. **Restraint:** darkness and negative space dominate; fire is an event in the field, not a wall.
8. **Emission tied to motion:** fire is laid down along the path of the invisible force and inherits
   its velocity; buoyancy takes over as that momentum decays.

## The three studies

- **D · Directed ribbon:** one ribbon of fire is gathered, launched, coils once and is released,
  then rises and cools.
- **E · Concentrated heat:** a thin seam of extreme heat; the air shimmers and motes ignite near it;
  restrained flares rise and collapse.
- **F · Converging bursts:** two directed bursts curve towards each other, meet in a brief flare, and
  the merged energy rises and dissipates into embers.
